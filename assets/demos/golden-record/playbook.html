“People do not always appear exactly the same in data. Entity Resolution behaves like a careful investigator: it combines several clues and produces a confidence level.”

How it works

  1. Blocking: quickly finds plausible candidates instead of comparing every row with every other row.
  2. Feature comparison: measures name, email, phone, date and address similarity.
  3. Match probability: estimates how likely two records describe the same person.
  4. Clustering: connects all related records into one identity group.

Confidence score

Confidence combines source coverage, agreement between records and completeness of important attributes. High confidence means stronger evidence—not that the person is low risk.

07

“Risk” has several meanings—keep them separate

A trustworthy record can still represent a high-risk customer. Data confidence and customer risk answer different questions.

Reliable data enables better risk decisions

Four risk views in the demo

Data Quality Risk

Is the source record incomplete, invalid or inconsistent?

Identity Risk

Could we merge two different people or miss a duplicate?

Customer Risk

Does the customer have low, medium, high or critical business risk?

Compliance Risk

Is KYC expired? Is the customer a PEP or sanctioned?

Example: Rachel can have a 93% Golden Record confidence while holding a WATCH risk rating. This means the platform is highly confident about who Rachel is and what the source systems say; it does not mean Rachel is automatically low risk.
“Confidence measures our trust in the data. Risk rating measures the business or compliance concern associated with the customer. Never present those as the same score.”

Dashboard metrics in plain English

  • LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL: portfolio distribution by customer risk.
  • KYC VERIFIED: identity checks are current and accepted.
  • KYC PENDING: verification is still in progress.
  • KYC FAILED: verification did not meet requirements.
  • KYC EXPIRED: the customer needs re-verification.
  • PEP: politically exposed person requiring enhanced attention.
  • Sanctioned: matched to a sanctions indicator and subject to regulatory control.

What its metrics mean

  • Sources: record chunks used to support the answer.
  • Similarity score: how closely a retrieved chunk matches the question.
  • Confidence: average retrieval relevance, not legal certainty.
  • Fact-checked badge: the answer has sufficient word overlap with the retrieved context.
  • Latency: time taken to retrieve and prepare the response.
  • Query history: audit trail of who asked what and what evidence was used.
Best demo practice

11

The same data is viewed through two different safety lenses

A Compliance Officer needs internal risk evidence. A customer should see only their own appropriate information.

Right answer, right audience

Persona comparison

Internal · Compliance Officer

Can retrieve risk rating, confidence, PEP, sanctions, KYC and source metadata. Answers are framed for investigation and compliance review.

Customer · Self Service

Receives friendly answers about their profile and verification. Internal risk, confidence, PEP and sanctions information is filtered out.

Customer asks: What is my KYC status?
Your identity verification is currently complete. If updated documents are needed, your bank will contact you.Customer-safe response · internal risk fields hidden
“Persona filtering is like having two doors to the same secure records room. Employees with a compliance purpose enter through one door; customers enter through another and see only what is appropriate.”

What the customer must never see

  • Internal risk score or rating.
  • PEP investigation details.
  • Sanctions screening logic.
  • Source-system names and confidence calculations.
  • Other customers’ information.
What they can see
  • Their name and contact details.
  • Their KYC or verification status.
  • Appropriate account/profile information.
  • Guidance to contact a relationship manager when data is restricted.
