Europe's data leaders are being asked to do three hard things at once: keep GDPR controls audit-ready, stand up governance for the EU AI Act as it comes into force, and meet DORA's operational-resilience expectations in financial services. The common thread is that policy is no longer enough — regulators and auditors want evidence the controls actually ran.

Why I can lead this — GDPR controls I have delivered vendor-side

Most candidates relocating into Europe know GDPR from an exam. I have delivered GDPR-aligned controls in regulated production for global financial-services clients — vendor-side, not as an EU controller. At FICO I owned data-technology control governance across metadata, quality, privacy and reliability for 50+ regulated financial-services clients — protecting 232M+ records per cycle with classification, masking, access control and audit-ready evidence. GDPR is not a framework I am learning for this market; it is the regulation I have operated under. The gap is European residence, not European regulation — and I am relocating and visa-ready.

The EU regimes, against controls I have already built:

  • GDPR — lawful basis, data-subject rights, classification, masking, retention and breach evidence — delivered vendor-side for global financial-services clients.
  • EU AI Act — data governance for high-risk systems, record-keeping/logging, human oversight and model controls — the exact control families I built into governed AI.
  • DORA — incident management, RCA, control testing and audit evidence: the operational-resilience discipline behind my 95% incident-reduction and worst-case resolution cut from 20–100 hours to under 4 at FICO.
  • DAMA-DMBOK — the ownership, metadata, lineage and quality backbone, held as a CDMP credential (Governance 83%, Data Quality 88%).

The EU AI Act, as working software — not a policy deck

The AI Act's obligations for high-risk systems map cleanly onto controls I have built and can demonstrate live, rather than describe:

  • Data and data governance (Art. 10): privacy classification, survivorship and lineage — see Golden Record RAG resolving 89,198 source records into trusted golden records.
  • Record-keeping & logging (Art. 12): an immutable audit packet for every answer — see MediGovern RAG's cited, audit-logged responses.
  • Human oversight (Art. 14): review gates and explicit abstention when evidence conflicts — built into MediGovern's six-rule quality gate.
  • Transparency & control plane: ingestion, DQ, lineage and governance as operable screens — see Pipeline Pulse, DMBOK as working software.
